# Executive Compensation Policy

*Classification: Restricted — Admin (HR Leadership / Compensation Committee) only.*

## Purpose

This policy defines how compensation is structured for Acme Financial Services executives (Vice President level and above), overseen by the Board Compensation Committee.

## Compensation Components

Executive compensation consists of three components: base salary, an annual cash incentive (target 40–100% of base salary depending on level), and long-term equity incentives vesting over 4 years with a 1-year cliff.

## Annual Incentive Determination

The annual cash incentive is determined by a blend of company financial performance (60% weight — revenue and operating margin targets set by the Board) and individual performance rating (40% weight) from the standard Performance Review Process, with a maximum payout capped at 200% of target.

## Equity Grants

Initial equity grants for new executive hires are approved by the Compensation Committee and typically range from 0.05% to 0.5% of fully diluted shares depending on role and level, subject to a 4-year vesting schedule with a 25% cliff at year one and monthly vesting thereafter.

## Severance

Executives at the SVP level and above are eligible for a severance package of 12 months' base salary plus prorated annual incentive if terminated without cause, contingent on signing a separation and release agreement. A "double-trigger" provision extends this to accelerated equity vesting only if termination follows a change-in-control event within 12 months.

## Say-on-Pay and Disclosure

Executive compensation philosophy (though not individual figures for non-public companies) is reviewed annually by the Compensation Committee and summarized for the Board; individual compensation figures are Restricted data under the Data Classification Policy and must never be shared outside HR Leadership, Finance Leadership, and the Compensation Committee.

## Clawback Provision

Incentive compensation paid to an executive is subject to clawback if it was based on financial results that are later restated due to material non-compliance with financial reporting requirements, or if the executive engaged in misconduct that contributed to the need for the restatement.
