# Procurement Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how Acme Financial Services sources goods and services and selects vendors, working in conjunction with the Vendor Management Policy (which governs vendor risk review) and the Finance Expense Approval Policy (which governs spending thresholds).

## Competitive Bidding

Purchases expected to exceed $50,000 annually require at least 3 competitive bids unless a valid sole-source justification is documented and approved by the Head of Procurement. Bids are evaluated on cost, quality, delivery timeline, and vendor risk tier.

## Preferred Vendor List

Procurement maintains a Preferred Vendor List for common categories (software, office supplies, travel, contractors). Sourcing from a preferred vendor does not eliminate the security or Finance approval requirements defined elsewhere, but simplifies onboarding.

## Contracts

All vendor contracts must be reviewed by Legal before signature. Standard contract templates pre-approved by Legal may be used for purchases under $25,000 without a full Legal review cycle, provided no terms are modified.

## New Vendor Intake

A new vendor cannot be paid until it has been entered into the vendor master file, which requires a completed W-9 (or equivalent), banking details for ACH payment, and — for Tier 1/Tier 2 vendors — completion of the Vendor Management Policy's security review.

## Software Purchases

All new software purchases, regardless of dollar amount, require IT and Security review before purchase to confirm compatibility, licensing terms, and data handling implications, in coordination with the Acceptable Use Policy's approved software catalog.

## Sustainability and Diversity

Where cost and quality are comparable among bidders, Procurement gives preference to vendors with verified sustainability certifications or supplier diversity credentials, consistent with Acme FS's public ESG commitments.

## Emergency Procurement

In a business-critical emergency (e.g., replacing failed production infrastructure), the Head of Procurement may authorize an expedited purchase without competitive bidding, but must document the justification and obtain CFO ratification within 5 business days after the fact.
